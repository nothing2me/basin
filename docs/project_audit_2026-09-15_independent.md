# BASIN hackathon audit — 15 September 2026

## Judgment and scope

**BASIN is for a rural-serving water provider or regional participant to choose which hypothetical rainfall drought patterns deserve professional modeling and to hand those patterns off with their assumptions and source records.** It is a rainfall screening workbench. It does not calculate a defensible irrigation order, county burn ban, water supply, safe yield, or reservoir restriction date.

This is an independent assessment of the current working tree, including uncommitted edits. It uses the official judging criteria in *UPDATED Official Rules (8.10.26).pdf* and the five design principles in *Updated Resource Packet (8.10.26).pdf*. The supplied pasted template shaped the questions; its instructions are not the hackathon rules or authority to change or submit anything. The official rules give **no numerical weights** for the criteria and permit stage-specific guidance. The numbers below are my estimates for this build, not Zoho's score. I inspected source, tests, current documentation, a live local browser run at 1280 px width, the supplied packets, and an existing five-page report rendering. I did not interview a district operator or validate the final presentation device. I did not change product source.

| Criterion | Estimate / 10 | Evidence and limit |
|---|---:|---|
| Impact | 6 | The Region N need is specific: the water-supply model uses hydrology through 2015 and expert modeling is costly. A reviewable rainfall shortlist can focus a discussion. There is no measured reduction in staff time or demonstrated improvement in decisions. |
| Feasibility | 6 | Deterministic local core and verified bundled-NOAA export are credible. Python 3.12 installation, an optional multi-gigabyte model, slow report rendering here, and the broken local-gauge export reduce deployability for a district without IT support. |
| Community Centeredness | 4 | Users can change priorities, notes, scenario rainfall, review decisions, and consent. Only three consenting discovery responses are recorded; the intended-user exercise and independent handoff are pending. The central “use my gauge” export fails. |
| Innovation | 6 | The combination of whole-window rainfall scenarios, grouping, editable shortlist, evidence disagreements, and replayable handoff is more than a static dashboard. KMeans and weighted ranking themselves are standard, and the crop/fire panels add little novel decision value. |
| Clarity | 4 | The workflow has real explanations and units, yet the crop and fire callouts overclaim, the default builder's scope is misstated, and report language treats an arbitrary five-window sample as statistically robust. |

**Indicative total: 26/50.** A finalist-worthy software prototype exists, but it is not yet a rural decision tool that a skeptical operator could safely adopt without a hydrologist beside them. The weakest criterion is Community Centeredness, closely followed by Clarity. The single highest-leverage change is a **reviewed local-gauge workflow that survives restart and exports/replays correctly**, tested uncoached with one rural-serving provider. That would turn user control from a UI assertion into usable practice.

## What the project actually processes

The bundled source is three NOAA GHCN-Daily airport-station precipitation series, 1991–2025, in millimeters, checked against a SHA-256 snapshot manifest. The reference uses 1991–2020 monthly means, with matched historical windows ending by 2015 for the pre-2016 rainfall comparison. The generator selects complete simultaneous historical windows and multiplies their daily rainfall by stated retention fractions; it does not transform precipitation into streamflow. KMeans groups scenarios by features; fixed weighted components rank them; a shortlist takes group representatives. Review can attach evidence, record conflicts, edit rainfall, include/exclude revisions, and save notes. The ZIP exports daily rainfall, assumptions, audit data, and a verifier. Its hashes and replay check internal consistency, not source authenticity, hydrologic validity, or signed approval.

The optional storage experiment supplies assumed capacity, rainfall-to-inflow coefficients, evaporation, demand, and response bands. It conserves quantities in its own arithmetic but is uncalibrated. The crop panel uses fixed monthly ETo normals and crop coefficients, subtracting rainfall day by day. The fire panel applies a KBDI-like calculation using fixed monthly temperature assumptions and a selected initial index. Those two panels share the scenario rainfall input, but neither is tied to field soil moisture, a verified source area, an adopted policy, or the district's real operations.

The professional baseline matters. The [Region N technical memorandum](https://www.twdb.texas.gov/waterplanning/rwp/planningdocu/2026/projectdocs/Tech_Memos/RegionN_TechnicalMemorandum.pdf) distinguishes the Corpus Christi Water Supply Model from Nueces WAM Run 3 and describes system operations, evaporation, naturalized flows, and a multi-year drought; an airport-rainfall proxy cannot replace those. The [Texas ET Grower's Guide](https://texaset.tamu.edu/Resources/Documents/Grower's%20Guide.pdf) uses stage-specific crop coefficients, *effective* rainfall, field conditions, and irrigation efficiency. [Drought.gov's USFS KBDI reference](https://www.drought.gov/data-maps-tools/keetch-byram-drought-index) calls for daily maximum temperature and 24-hour rain, while the [Texas A&M Forest Service](https://tfsweb.tamu.edu/wildfire-and-other-disasters/burn-bans-and-information/) says Texas burn bans are local-government decisions. Existing data portals include [TWDB regional water plans](https://www.twdb.texas.gov/waterplanning/rwp/plans/2026/index.asp), [Texas ET](https://texaset.tamu.edu/), and the Forest Service's current burn-ban and KBDI products. BASIN's distinct value must be choosing and documenting a defensible *next modeling question*, not restating those portals' numbers or replacing their live operational guidance.

## Screen-by-screen value and content classification

Here **Critical** means essential for safe use and confirmed present where stated; **Valuable** means it helps the screening task; **Noise** means it consumes attention without enough interpretive value; **Misleading** means a plausible operator could take it too literally. This classifies the prominent current components I inspected, not every hidden accessibility element or every advanced widget.

| Screen / component | Class | Value test and action |
|---|---|---|
| Data: station snapshot, flags, dates, completeness, checksum | **Critical, present** | A reviewer needs source and coverage before comparing scenarios. Move the fact that these are airport proxies beside the first map/selection, not inside a later disclosure. The checksum proves byte identity only. |
| Data: map and 1991–2025 rainfall chart | **Valuable** | It orients the region and displays history. Label the stations' geographic relationship to source catchments; an attractive map does not establish catchment suitability. |
| Data: local CSV parser, explicit unit, gaps, paired-day comparison | **Valuable** | Parsing and explicit comparisons improve on a raw file. Say upfront that activation has separate persistence/export limits. The comparison is not a seasonal normal or source-validation verdict. |
| Data: “Data Sovereignty: Use in Scenario Generator” and activation | **Misleading** | It promises a handoff driven by a local gauge, but the current export verifier rejects that run and a normal reopen lacks the gauge. Repair before leading with the promise. |
| Workspace: chosen stations, source dates, retained-rainfall range, scenario count | **Critical, present** | These determine what is generated. The ordinary date picker currently fixes one source window; show “variations of this selected record” as the result. Provide a separate explicit historical-window sampling option if that is the intended analysis. |
| Workspace: ranking weights, sensitivity, shortlist comparison | **Valuable** | A provider can expose priorities and compare candidate patterns. However, weights are tucked under Advanced ranking, and initial generation uses defaults; put priorities before the shortlist is built. |
| Workspace: “Scenarios to test” 100–1000 and groups | **Noise in the one-window mode** | Hundreds of multipliers and cluster names can make one record look like a broad historical screen. Show the count of unique historical source windows, durations, and seasons beside the candidate count. |
| Review: source window, retention, shortfall with units, comparison plot | **Critical, present** | These let a skeptic trace the constructed input. The 25-window reference count in the live example helps; the result still depends on proxy-station suitability. |
| Review: “Historical Severity ≥100%” and concurrence cards | **Valuable with correction** | The rank is relative to matched rainfall windows, not the historical drought of record or an occurrence probability. “Widespread stress” overstates three regional station proxies; use “selected stations stressed together.” |
| Review: include/exclude, revision invalidation, evidence conflicts | **Critical, present** | Human disposition and audit history are the strongest part of the product. The export page's “Accept all shortlisted” weakens deliberation; require a reason for batch inclusion or label it an explicit demo shortcut. |
| Review: storage trajectory and band countdowns | **Misleading when foregrounded** | Assumptions are disclosed, but threshold days and “Response Band Crossings” take visual priority over a source-area warning. Keep the experiment subordinate to the rainfall review and put “uncalibrated, no restriction date” in the chart title/callout. |
| Review: crop “Net Irrigation Deficit” and claim of depth required to prevent yield reduction | **Misleading** | It is a toy daily ETo-minus-total-rain metric, not the field's irrigation requirement. Remove the yield claim and either build a field balance with effective rain, soil storage, crop stage, and application efficiency or retain it as a labeled rainfall–ET illustration. |
| Review: KBDI “Burn-Ban Trigger,” green below-600 / red breached cards | **Misleading** | Fixed monthly temperatures and airport rainfall cannot determine a county burn ban. Replace the legal-sounding trigger with “illustrative KBDI stress marker”; link to current county status, with an offline last-updated date when available. |
| Exports: ZIP replay and privacy opt-ins | **Critical, present for bundled NOAA** | A synthetic bundled-NOAA run exported and replayed successfully. Consent changes clear the live packet; on-disk saved downloads bypass that freshness gate. A recipient needs a visible statement of exactly which artifact was checked and when. |
| Exports: PDF executive summary, four KPI boxes, 35%/15% comparative finding | **Misleading / Noise mix** | The five-page PDF is legible, but the top page sells modeled threshold timing before model suitability. The fixed 35%/15% example can be mistaken for the user's tested action; separate it from reviewed scenario conclusions or remove it. Four equal cards imply equal certainty. |
| Exports: environmental footprint tab | **Valuable but incomplete** | It discloses timing, estimated 15–65 W power, and unquantified water/lifecycle cost. Call the energy a scenario estimate, not measured consumption; measure a representative no-LLM run and optional-model run if claimed. |
| Assistant drawer | **Minor value, optional** | Deterministic question tools may aid explanation. The optional Qwen model adds weight, setup, and energy uncertainty to a workflow that does not need prose inference for correctness. Keep the core demo independent of it. |

The first 30 seconds would raise three questions: “Are these airport gauges actually upstream of my supply?”, “Why is 20.66 inches called the water I must apply to prevent loss?”, and “Why is a computed 600 a burn-ban trigger?” The proxy warning is scientifically honest in documentation, but the interface lets its most prominent numbers outrun that warning. A farm operator seeking a field irrigation decision would close the tab after seeing no field soil, irrigation system, or current water allotment; the correct audience is a provider choosing questions for technical review.

## Design principles from the brief

| Principle | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Community sovereignty | **Partial.** Local operation, edit/reject controls, priority weights, and private-by-default notes are real. Community partnership is unproven: the three-response discovery sample is narrow, no uncoached provider exercise is recorded, and gauge-based replay fails. “Community priority” presets are illustrative, not endorsed by communities. |
| Appropriate scale | **Mostly met by core.** Deterministic Python and local KMeans are fitting for the task. The optional ~2 GB Qwen weights, WebView/Chromium report path, and bulky report generation should be justified by demonstrated time saved. |
| Interoperability | **Partial.** Water, crops, and fire use the same hypothetical rainfall, which is a connection in code. There is no joint decision or shared soil-water/supply representation, and CSV handoff to a hydrologist has not been independently exercised. The Region N memorandum cautions against assuming Nueces WAM Run 3 is the Corpus Christi operations model. |
| Equity of access | **Partial.** Offline core is valuable; setup requires Python 3.12 x64 and pinned wheels, and the final laptop/offline workflow is pending. The app still speaks in “engineering objective,” percentiles, score components, and pseudo-operational trigger language. |
| Honest environmental accounting | **Partial.** The UI names estimated power and unquantified water cost, unlike the blank claimed in the pasted template. No power meter or water-footprint measurement is recorded. Do not describe estimates as measured savings. |

## Prioritized findings

| Severity | Location | Issue | Why it matters to a rural-serving operator | Suggested fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Blocker** | Data activation → local-gauge ZIP | A synthetic gauge produced scenarios, then `export_bundle` / replay failed with `ValueError: Snapshot stations do not match the manifest registry`. The manifest adds a local station while bundled raw snapshot bytes do not. | The community-controlled input cannot complete the advertised handoff. | Store and serialize the local station with its normalized records and source identity, update the verifier's source contract, then replay a genuine activated-gauge bundle. Keep original/private bytes behind explicit consent. |
| **Blocker** | Saved Runs → open local-gauge analysis | A saved synthetic-gauge session could not reopen against the bundled source: `Saved session requires station(s) absent from source snapshot: LOCAL_AUDIT`. The Saved Runs UI loads the bundled source rather than reconstructing the custom one. | An operator can lose access to work after restarting. | Restore the local gauge deterministically from consented, versioned session data or keep a persistent local source registry; verify byte/normalization identity before opening. |
| **Blocker** | Exports → “Download Saved PDF/ZIP” | The live packet is cleared when inputs/consent change, but the fallback buttons read existing disk files whenever they exist, without the same current fingerprint or consent check. This is confirmed in source; direct browser download after revocation was not exercised. | A previously notes-inclusive or outdated brief can be handed off as if current. | Check disk artifact metadata against the current run fingerprint and privacy choice; hide stale downloads and mark old local files as historical drafts. |
| **Major** | Crop deficit calculation and review callout | The UI says the computed 20.66 in is supplemental irrigation “required across crop cycle to prevent yield reduction.” Code sums `max(ETc_daily - rain_daily, 0)` with fixed monthly ETo/Kc and no effective rainfall, soil-water carryover, efficiency, field location, or crop-stage record. A two-day synthetic case with 1 inch on day one and no rain on day two reports 0.27 inch irrigation even though total rain exceeds total ET; that arithmetic is plausible as an illustrative daily imbalance, not a field requirement. | A farm could interpret an unvalidated depth as an instruction to irrigate. | Remove “required” and “prevent yield reduction”; supply a field-calibrated soil-water balance and clear uncertainty only if this becomes operational. |
| **Major** | KBDI method and burn-ban cards | Code uses monthly fixed temperature approximations and a fixed 32-in annual rain assumption, while the cards say “Burn-Ban Trigger” and “Critical threshold crossed.” Source research shows KBDI needs daily maximum temperature and a county ban is a local-government action. One UI warning also says KBDI “reaches 658 on Day 50,” while the takeaway says it peaks on Day 73; Day 50 is the crossing, not the peak. | It can confuse modeled drought stress with a legal restriction and a real date. | Rename the marker, fix crossing-versus-peak wording, show missing temperature inputs, and route legal status to the official county source. |
| **Major** | Workspace date picker and generation | The ordinary builder's date range becomes an exact source interval. In a 30-candidate synthetic NOAA run, all candidates used **one** 90-day source window and one season. Help copy says candidates vary across the 1991–2025 record and Review calls the result historical analogs. | Count and clusters suggest wider evidence than was sampled. | Make “one selected source record” explicit, or provide a separate matched-window search that actually samples historical years and reports unique source windows. |
| **Major** | Initial community priorities | Initial `Workspace` applies default weights before the user sees Advanced ranking controls; later preset changes rerank but do not rebuild the shortlist without a separate action. | A provider may think its stated priorities made the suggested six when they did not. | Ask priorities before initial selection, or immediately rebuild with a clearly recorded before/after reason. |
| **Major** | PDF terminology glossary | Existing five-page output includes literal “(6–10 words)” in the concurrence definition and claims `n ≥ 5` is the minimum for “robust statistical significance.” Five matched windows are a minimum gating rule, not a general significance guarantee. The PDF also labels concurrence a “regional share,” while the core measure is a fraction of eligible **days/windows** with selected stations stressed. | The most shareable artifact misstates a statistical and spatial claim. | Remove placeholder words, define the denominator correctly, and report sample count and empirical rank without significance language. |
| **Major** | Source geography and storage assumptions | Three airport gauges are weighted equally; no source-catchment fit or rainfall–runoff calibration is established. A preset uses a linear rainfall-to-inflow coefficient and fixed evaporation/demand. The prominent PDF puts modeled band timings and a 35%/15% comparison on page one. | A rural provider needs to know whether the input represents its water source before hearing a “Day 151” number. | Make the evidence packet's first decision “which catchment/gauge is suitable?”; keep storage illustrations outside the primary shortlist verdict until a hydrologist reviews fit and coefficients. |
| **Major** | Community validation and recipient handoff | Only three anonymous discovery responses are recorded. The intended-user exercise, practitioner station review, final-laptop install, and independent CSV import remain pending in project documentation. | Community control and usefulness are still hypotheses. | Run one uncoached task with a rural provider and one hydrologist recipient; record time, errors, rejected scenario rationale, and whether the CSV answered a concrete modeling request. |
| **Major** | Exports bulk acceptance | “Accept all shortlisted for export” adds a standard note to every scenario and unlocks the packet without individual examination. The live export page offered this action after a six-scenario example. | It makes the human-review gate look stronger than the interaction warrants. | Require a batch rationale with explicit scope, or keep the button only in labeled demo mode; use “included by batch decision,” not “reviewed,” in outputs. |
| **Major** | Report generation in the live app | The exports page renders HTML/chart images even for inactive preview tabs; Plotly-to-image calls invoke a browser renderer. The local exports page repeatedly stayed in “Running…” after consent/note changes, and tests with report paths made slow progress. This is local observation, not a measured failure rate on the presentation laptop. | A short hackathon demo or low-spec district laptop can stall on a secondary preview. | Generate report images only on request, cache by scenario/config fingerprint, time out image generation, and keep a readable vector/text report path. |
| **Minor** | Footprint and assistant | The app estimates energy from 15–65 W × elapsed time and says water footprint is unquantified. Optional local model weights and runtime have separate setup needs. | Judges asked for honest compute and water accounting. | Measure a representative no-model run on the target laptop; state meter/method, setup and inference energy separately, and keep unknown water cost explicit. |
| **Minor** | Review visual hierarchy | Four equally sized KPI cards, icons, dense title jargon, and many report figures compete with the primary question of which scenario merits modeling. The first PDF page visually favors depletion and a fixed benchmark. | A no-staff operator must decode the display before understanding the decision. | Lead each screen with “Which scenario and why?”, source suitability, and one next step; move speculative metrics behind Advanced View. |

## Verification and confidence

The synthetic local-gauge reproduction generated candidates but failed verified export and bundled-source reopen as quoted above. The same reproduction produced a bundled-NOAA packet with `verified: true` and three accepted scenarios; that proves the **declared verifier scope**, not physical validity. It also demonstrated the single-window builder behavior and the daily crop-balance caveat. A local browser example showed Review and Export controls, agriculture and wildfire callouts, and a six-candidate batch-review path. The existing five-page PDF rendered cleanly on page one and exposed the terminology claim; I did not treat its visual polish as scientific evidence.

The repository's recorded acceptance matrix itself marks an uncoached intended-user exercise, final laptop, projector, and organizer format as unrun. The focused core test run passed **162 tests in 174.61 seconds**. Two broader pytest invocations were stopped after report/render paths ran slowly, so I do **not** claim a full-suite pass. No test result substitutes for the direct reproductions or practitioner review. Source references: `app.py`, `basin_core/data.py`, `basin_core/engine.py`, `basin_core/agronomics.py`, `basin_core/exporter.py`, `basin_core/pdf_report.py`, `basin_core/workspace.py`, `docs/validation_notes.md`, `docs/device_acceptance_results.md`.

**Verdict:** BASIN has a defensible, useful center: deterministic rainfall scenarios with exposed assumptions, editable human decisions, conflict history, and an auditable NOAA handoff. It is neither an empty shell nor an adoptable operational tool yet. Its rural/community claim is most vulnerable where it should be strongest: a local operator's own gauge cannot complete the verified workflow, and the prominent crop/fire/storage language implies decisions that the inputs cannot support. Fix the local-data handoff, narrow the first screen and brief to “which rainfall patterns should a hydrologist model,” and show a real provider completing that task. Those changes would give judges evidence of community partnership and a clearer reason to trust the innovation.
