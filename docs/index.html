Technical Documentation

Hydrologic rigor & statutory compliance.

Designed to bridge the gap between complex Fortran water availability models and rapid executive decision-making.

Finite-Difference Continuity Formulation

Reservoir storage trajectories are solved via an explicit forward finite-difference scheme. Net surface evaporation is coupled to storage via the instantaneous surface area polynomial:

E_t = A(S_t) * (e_pan * k_c - P_t) A(S_t) = c_0 + c_1 * S_t + c_2 * S_t^2 Where: E_t = Net volumetric evaporative loss (Acre-Feet) A(S) = Reservoir water surface area (Acres) e_pan = Pan evaporation rate (Feet/month) k_c = Monthly lake-to-pan pan coefficient P_t = Direct precipitation on lake surface (Feet)

This eliminates the common Excel spreadsheet error where evaporative losses are computed against static surface areas during severe multi-year drawdowns.

Dual-Reservoir Joint Operating Rules

In the Lower Nueces River Basin, Choke Canyon Reservoir (1,143,000 AF normal pool) and Lake Corpus Christi (256,000 AF normal pool) operate under a joint system agreement:

Rule 1: Lake Corpus Christi drawdown priority - Downstream releases sourced primarily from LCC - Minimizes high surface-area evaporative losses Rule 2: Choke Canyon emergency transfer trigger - Triggered when LCC capacity drops < 30% - Gravity conveyance routing: Q_choke -> Q_nueces_river Rule 3: Combined critical storage curtailment - Joint capacity < 40% -> Municipal Stage 2 - Joint capacity < 30% -> Municipal Stage 3 - Joint capacity < 20% -> Stage 4 Industrial Curtailment
