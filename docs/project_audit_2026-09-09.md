# BASIN design and functionality audit

Audit date: September 9, 2026, America/Chicago. Source revision: `c8683907949720ffbbf219ed27f53dc2e0c02473`. Audience: rural water district operators and small/mid-size farm operators. Evaluation framework: the five judging criteria and five design principles supplied in the request. No numerical judging weights were supplied; the ratings below are audit judgments, not official scores.

**BASIN is not ready to support real irrigation, burning, allocation, or infrastructure decisions.** It has a substantive rainfall evidence and replay engine, but recent operational features undermine that engine's careful boundaries. The most serious issues are incorrect burn-ban authority, an inadequate irrigation calculation, broken local-gauge provenance and recovery, and reports that can describe the wrong infrastructure or remain downloadable after becoming stale.

From the user's point of view, the defensible purpose is: **“Help me choose and explain historical rainfall stress examples, attach my local evidence, and hand a reproducible packet to someone who can assess water-supply consequences.”** This is a preparation task. The interface increasingly promises the consequences themselves without the observations or models to establish them.

## 1. What the code actually does

| Layer | Actual implementation | Decision boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Public observations | `scripts/fetch_noaa.py` reads GHCN-Daily PRCP for Corpus Christi, Victoria, and San Antonio airport stations, restricted to 1991–2025. It converts tenths of mm, excludes missing/negative values, quality failures, and missing-presumed-zero flags, preserves trace flags, and writes a hashed snapshot. | Historical point rainfall, not catchment rainfall, current storage, groundwater availability, or inflow. |
| Runtime source | `CachedSource` verifies CSV hash, station registry, dates, duplicate dates, and nonnegative finite observations; it maintains calendar gaps. The bundled record has one missing day at Victoria, one at San Antonio, and none at Corpus Christi. | Appropriate checks of internal consistency; no sensor calibration or geographic validation. |
| Scenarios | `ScenarioGenerator` selects complete synchronized historical windows, selected onset months and durations, and applies rainfall-retention factors. Dates remain historical source dates. | Deliberately constructed stress tests. The candidate count is not an ensemble probability distribution. |
| Features | Monthly 1991–2020 expected rainfall, equal-station clipped net deficits, matched historical percentile, rolling 30-day simultaneous station stress, dry spells, and summer timing. | Useful descriptive rainfall comparisons for the NOAA path. Local-gauge fallback violates the displayed reference contract. |
| Selection | Fixed feature scaling, local KMeans, normalized weighted ranking, group representatives, manual selection/replacement, and alternative-weight comparisons. | Organizes examples and exposes preferences; does not learn water-system performance or predict drought likelihood. |
| Reviewed evidence | Public evidence records, disagreements and human dispositions, rainfall revisions, and exact-revision acceptance. A separate custom-evidence workflow stores original bytes locally and versioned normalized records. | Real traceability and user agency. Acceptance is a content decision, not professional certification. |
| Newly activated gauges | `with_custom_station` augments an in-memory dataframe and station manifest but retains the original NOAA CSV and digest. A new scenario workspace is built directly from that source. | This path is not connected to the complete persistence/export contract and produces misleading baseline labels. |
| Storage experiment | Daily balance with assumed capacities, linear rainfall-to-inflow coefficients, seasonal evaporation, demand, and conservation. Municipal, farm-pond, regional, and custom configurations are available. | Accounting consistency is useful, but does not establish calibrated inflow, transferable evaporation, water rights, or restriction timing. |
| Farm/fire extensions | Fixed monthly ETo values and crop coefficients; daily positive ET-minus-rain sums; KBDI using assumed monthly temperatures, starting index, and annual rainfall. | Shared rainfall input is a real computational connection. The outputs are not validated farm water balance or operational fire guidance. |
| Exports | Replay ZIP, readable Markdown, PDF, and Excel. The PDF independently runs one selected/default scenario's storage sensitivity. | ZIP verification has a defined scope. The PDF and Excel do not inherit that verification. |
| Assistant | Direct deterministic tools and intent routing, with an application-owned Qwen2.5-3B worker if weights/runtime are available. | Current source differs from the deterministic-only README. I did not benchmark generated answers or load the model during the browser audit. |

## 2. Baseline against established tools

This comparison is about capabilities and decision requirements, not a claim that every rural operator uses each named tool.

| Existing reference/tool | What a trustworthy workflow provides | BASIN implication |
|---|---|---|
| TexasET and California CIMIS | Weather-based reference ET, appropriate crop coefficients, and irrigation guidance grounded in site conditions. TexasET provides crop-specific coefficient guidance; CIMIS explains replenishment and irrigation efficiency. | Displaying ETc alone is insufficient to prescribe how much water to apply. Source the local ETo series and crop stage explicitly. [TexasET cotton guidance](https://texaset.tamu.edu/Static/CottonCoef), [CIMIS resources](https://cimis.water.ca.gov/Resources.aspx?t=1). |
| FAO irrigation methods/CROPWAT | Crop, climate, soil, and effective rainfall enter water requirements and scheduling. Rainfall that runs off or drains below roots differs from water retained for crop use. | BASIN lacks the soil-water state, rooting depth, effective-rainfall treatment, and efficiency needed for its “Net Irrigation Deficit” label. [FAO irrigation water needs](https://www.fao.org/4/s2022e/s2022e08.htm), [FAO ETc methods](https://www.fao.org/4/X0490E/x0490e0a.htm), [FAO software catalog](https://www.fao.org/land-water/resources/tools/software/en). |
| TWDB Water Data for Texas | Timestamped observed conservation storage/capacity and percent-full definitions. | A preset 48% starting level is not a local district's measured condition. BASIN needs explicit observed-versus-assumed status and the applicable storage definition. [Water Data for Texas](https://waterdatafortexas.org/reservoirs/statewide). |
| Texas A&M Forest Service | Actual burn-ban information is separate from KBDI and broader fire-potential assessment. County judges/commissioners can impose restrictions; KBDI products provide drought information. | A synthetic KBDI cannot announce a county ban. Weather and fuel conditions matter beyond the drought index. [Burn bans and information](https://tfsweb.tamu.edu/wildfire-and-other-disasters/burn-bans-and-information/). |
| KBDI source guidance | Daily maximum temperature affects drying; actual county/pixel values have specific geography and update times. | BASIN substitutes monthly mean temperatures and a user-selected starting index without matching operational input provenance. [KBDI FAQ](https://twc.tamu.edu/docs/KBDI_FAQ.pdf). |
| NOAA GHCN-Daily | Units, missing values, measurement/quality flags, station identity, and observation definitions are part of the data contract. | BASIN's original parser handles the principal PRCP units and flags appropriately. Keep that discipline in every derived chart and new upload path. [NOAA specification](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/pub/data/ghcn/daily/readme.txt). |

## 3. Value and first-use test, by screen

| Screen/view | User task | Does it outperform raw data or an existing tool? | First skeptical reaction / reason to leave |
|---|---|---|---|
| Welcome/Data | Establish whether the observations apply locally. | Partly. Bundled observations, provenance, quality checks, and offline viewing save preparation. The broad map and annual line chart mostly repackage public data. | “Which gauge represents my field or supply? Why is an airport several counties away relevant?” Local data entry is collapsed below the welcome content. |
| Upload preview/public comparison | Check my gauge file and compare it with a public reference. | Yes for validation and paired-date comparison. It preserves missing values and blocks undeclared comparison bases. The activation branch currently destroys that advantage through incomplete provenance. | “My current-year file previews, but why can't I build or reopen an analysis from it?” |
| Scenario Builder | Choose a small set of rainfall stress examples. | Yes for an evidence-preparation task. Diverse selection and explicit weights add interpretive value. No demonstrated time-saving result for rural operators yet. | “What settings should I choose, and what decision will 300 candidates change?” The screen opens with technical configuration, not the shortlist or a local question. |
| Compare scenarios/priorities | Understand why examples differ and how values affect selection. | Yes. It compares the same pool, records tradeoffs, and does not silently overwrite a shortlist. | Fractions, scores, group labels, and multiple controls still require instruction. “How unusual” can be mistaken for future risk. |
| Review rainfall | Compare a scenario with a reference and include/exclude a revision. | Yes. The paired reference chart, source dates, units, revision state, and edit history are useful. | The headline infers rarity and supply stress that the nearby limitations explicitly deny. |
| Evidence/conflicts | Explain disagreement and preserve local knowledge. | Yes, potentially the strongest distinctive feature. Evidence types and unresolved issues are meaningful. | Form-heavy, deep in expanders, no observed novice-use evidence. Technical forms and default authority language can replace genuine local judgment. |
| Storage experiment | Explore sensitivity to demand and rainfall assumptions. | Limited educational value. It is not an operational allocation tool. | “Where are my measured storage, inflows, evaporation, releases, pump limits, and district policy?” Entering a pond capacity silently manufactures other parameters. |
| Crop ET / wildfire | Decide irrigation needs or assess burning conditions. | No for operational use. The apparent interpretation is stronger than the model. | Incorrect net-irrigation and statutory-ban claims are enough to close the tool. |
| Assistant | Get a supported answer without finding a control. | Possibly useful for discovering deterministic tools. Crop shortcuts repeat invalid conclusions. No evidence the model justifies its added download/memory cost. | “Hydrologist Assistant” and “verified hydrologic tools” imply competence beyond validated rainfall calculations. |
| Exports/PDF/Excel | Share a stable, intelligible record. | ZIP replay is a real contribution. PDF/Excel are convenient, but report defects and stale downloads undermine the handoff. | “Why does my small district's report name the Corpus Christi system?” |

The new gauge cannot reliably resume after restart; deletion is too broad. | “Why are my analyses identified by timestamps/hashes, and can ‘purge drafts’ delete reviewed work?” |

“Just another dashboard” applies primarily to the Data annual plot without a selected task, the ornamental map treatment, duplicated summary cards, and the farm/fire KPI rows. It does not fairly describe the core evidence/revision/replay workflow.

## 5. Missing information and hidden assumptions

For an actual irrigation decision, the missing essentials are field/zone identity, planted area, crop stage/planting date, soil available-water capacity, rooting depth, dated soil-moisture/depletion state, effective rainfall/runoff and drainage assumptions, irrigation already applied, system efficiency, application rate, and available pumping/delivery limits. The operator needs a defensible amount and timing with a reason and an uncertainty range. BASIN currently cannot supply that from rainfall alone. This follows the soil/effective-rainfall distinction in the [FAO irrigation guidance](https://www.fao.org/4/s2022e/s2022e08.htm).

For a rural district, missing essentials include observed usable storage and its timestamp, dead storage/pump-intake limitations, measured inflow/outflow and losses, enforceable allocations and environmental releases, demand by relevant customer class, external supply reliability, local drought-plan version/authority, and uncertainty in the resulting balance. These are not optional improvements to a real restriction countdown. BASIN's own methodology correctly lists several as excluded.

For local rainfall, the new activation path needs the actual retained date range, independent source digest, original units/parser choices, station identifiers with collision resistance, observation-day definition, complete-window eligibility, missing-data rejection messages, and an honest reference-adequacy state. A short current record must not silently become a 35-year NOAA record with an empirical rarity score.

For fire decisions, the tool needs actual jurisdiction and order status/date, input weather/temperature provenance, index initialization/spin-up, local fuels and weather context, and an explicit statement that an unavailable or stale burn-ban lookup does not authorize burning. The Forest Service treats bans and broader fire potential as separate products. [Official source](https://tfsweb.tamu.edu/wildfire-and-other-disasters/burn-bans-and-information/).

For handoff, the missing essential is a precise artifact-level identity: scenario ID and revision actually simulated, infrastructure ID/capacity/parameters actually used, generation timestamp/timezone, source version, consent state, and whether the recipient is looking at the current review or a historical artifact. Every PDF/Excel/ZIP should make that status visible. A file's existence under a run ID does not establish it.

For community governance, the missing evidence is a completed operator trial, an independent recipient trial, a recorded change requested by a community partner, and a clear local retention/deletion/backup procedure. No cloud account system is required to fix this. Better local ownership and recovery are more appropriate than introducing an enterprise permissions system.

## 6. Design, readability, and generic AI-design signals

The main design problem is hierarchy and authority, not simply rounded corners.

- **Data:** the eye goes to the logo, large welcome pitch, four-step navigation, and map. It should go to “Do these stations and dates apply to my place?” Show station relevance/coverage and a conspicuous local-data task first. The recent download badge should show the final observation date just as prominently.
- **Builder:** two nearly identical questions precede two equally weighted forms. The useful shortlist summary is below them. Show the current shortlist and reason first; put seed, generation count, and advanced weights behind deliberate disclosure.
- **Review:** the first blue summary makes the strongest unsupported assertions. The actual decision controls are lower and to the side, while inclusion and bulk inclusion use the same oversized bright styling. Give the rainfall/reference chart and its limitation the first position; make inclusion/exclusion balanced, clear actions.
- **Storage:** the animated reservoir fill and trajectory convey precision without calibration. Static trajectories with a compact assumptions table are more useful on a low-powered machine and easier to print.
- **Exports:** three green success banners, a large PDF download, raw JSON, and multiple artifact types compete. There is a precise verification boundary in the JSON/PDF, but broad green copy overwhelms it. Use one status line, one primary rainfall packet, and a clearly scoped optional experiment appendix.
- **Navigation:** all four desktop nav buttons had the same computed foreground/background and no `aria-current`. The CSS assumes an HTML wrapper contains Streamlit's button, but the rendered buttons do not receive the intended active styling. Bind styles to actual Streamlit container keys and provide a semantic current-page cue.
- **Narrow view:** the first screen was mostly stacked header/navigation, while the side AI tab and notes dock remained fixed. This is a concrete access problem for small screens. Use compact navigation and avoid overlaying useful content.
- **Readability:** captions are configured around 0.78rem and metric labels around 0.76rem; PDF caveats reach 6.5pt. Large approval buttons do not compensate for small explanation text. No numeric WCAG contrast claim is made without a complete contrast audit.

Specific “AI slop” signals: “Data Sovereignty” branding above a broken source workflow; “Hydrologist Assistant Ready” and “verified hydrologic data” above unvalidated outputs; a fixed “0.01s” latency promise; “100% Baked-in Offline GIS” as a chart title; sparkles/rocket/lightning/megaphone icons attached to routine actions and authority claims; repeated green “verified” banners before and after verification; and uniform crop/fire/PDF metric cards that imply all displayed numbers have equal evidential standing. Replace these with input source, date, assumption, consequence, and user action. The professional-looking PDF deserves particular attention because “Resilient” and “Conservation Benefit” can travel beyond the caveats.

## Verdict

BASIN currently has a useful, substantially engineered rainfall evidence core surrounded by features that claim more operational knowledge than they possess. It is more than a styled shell, but it is not yet a tool a rural operator can safely adopt and fully control for the decisions named in the brief. Impact remains proposed, feasibility is strongest only in the deterministic core, innovation is most credible in traceable disagreement and replay, and clarity is damaged by conflicting claims. **Community Centeredness is the weakest criterion** because editable controls have not yet become a proven, durable workflow shaped and successfully used by the intended community. The highest-leverage change is to complete one partner-defined local-data-to-reviewed-handoff task, including correct source identity, restart/replay, honest limits and a recorded operator/recipient trial against their current process; that would improve all five criteria more than another AI feature.
